## What can I dispute, and how long do I have?

You can dispute a charge on your FinFlow account that you don't recognize, or a charge that was taken more than once. You may also see this described as being charged twice, a double charge, or an unauthorized or fraudulent charge. You have 60 days from the transaction date to open a dispute. After 60 days, you can no longer dispute the charge through the app.

## Can I dispute a transfer I sent to the wrong person?

No. A transfer you sent to the wrong person can't be disputed, because FinFlow can't force the recipient to return the money. Instead, a transfer sent to the wrong person is handled as a recall request, where FinFlow asks the recipient's bank to return the money. To start a recall request, email the support team with the transaction details and your account number. FinFlow will contact the recipient's bank, but the money only comes back if the recipient agrees to return it.

## How do I dispute a charge?

There are 4 steps to dispute a charge in the app:

1. Select the transaction you want to dispute.
2. Choose the reason for the dispute.
3. Add details or evidence, such as a screenshot or a description of what happened.
4. Submit the dispute.

You can open a dispute up to 60 days after the transaction date.

## How long does a dispute take, and do I get my money back?

A dispute takes 7-10 business days to review after you submit it. You don't get your money back while the dispute is being reviewed. If the dispute is upheld, the money returns to your FinFlow balance and you'll get a notification. If the dispute isn't upheld, the charge stays on your account and you'll get a notification explaining the decision. If you haven't received a decision within 10 business days of submitting your dispute, email the support team with the transaction details and your account number.

## Why was my account restricted after I reported a charge I didn't make?

If you report a transaction you didn't authorize, FinFlow may restrict your account to protect your money while the report is checked. A restricted account means you can log in and see your balance, but you can't make transactions. Your money is safe during the restriction, and you can still file and track a dispute, because filing doesn't move money. Your dispute is still reviewed within 7-10 business days of submitting it. If your dispute is upheld while your account is restricted, the money returns to your balance but stays frozen with the rest of your balance until the restriction is lifted. To find out what happens during a restriction and how it ends, read [Restricted account: what happens when FinFlow reviews your case]. It explains what a restriction means, what you can do in the meantime, and how long a review takes.
